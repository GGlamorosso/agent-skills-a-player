# Ontological Reasoning

## Inputs

- The exact question and requested output: explanation, decision, prediction, or plan.
- Relevant project reality, available sources, constraints, and unknowns.

## Procedure

1. Define the object, terms, scope, level, and time horizon.
2. Retrieve the smallest sufficient evidence set: project facts, best relevant model, independent perspective, and counter-model or boundary case when available.
3. Separate facts, named source claims, user assumptions, agent assumptions/inferences, and unknowns.
4. Model the entities/levels, functions, agents/actions, incentives/payoffs, constraints, information asymmetries, relationships, causal mechanisms, and state transitions.
5. Classify contradictions as empirical, definitional, causal, normative, contextual, or temporal. Preserve genuine incompatibilities.
6. Derive the conclusion from supported premises. Test the strongest rival model, second-order effects, boundary conditions, and falsifier.
7. Lead with the answer, then provide the minimum auditable causal chain, decisive contradiction or uncertainty, and concrete implication, test, or action.

## Output Contract

- Sources support premises; authority never replaces a causal bridge.
- Borrowed claims are attributed; original inferences are distinguishable.
- The result is neither a literature summary nor unconstrained speculation.
- Paraphrase may assist retrieval but is never the final analytical product.
- Use ontological or game-theory language only when it changes the model or decision.

For complex cases, read `references/protocol.md`.

## Quality Bar

Removing source names leaves a coherent derivation. Restoring them makes the evidence and assumptions auditable. The answer states what would change the conclusion when uncertainty is material.
